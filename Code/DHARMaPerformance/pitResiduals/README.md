# Calibration of PIT residuals (#653)

`pitResiduals.R` compares the PIT residuals of DHARMa 0.3.3 - 0.5.1 (k / nSim) with the randomized PIT residuals of DHARMa >= 0.5.2 under H0: rejection rates of the KS test and of the binomial outlier test, and outlier frequencies. Details and the run command are in the header of the script.

- `pitResiduals.csv`: summary table (committed). Produced with DHARMa 0.5.2 from branch i653-pitCalibration (PR #658), R 4.6.2, 300 replicates per scenario with n = 8000 observations, seeds set per replicate.
- `pitResiduals.rds`: raw results per replicate (not committed, regenerated by the script).

## Results and decisions (November 2026, PR #658)

**Question.** The PIT residuals of DHARMa 0.3.3 - 0.5.1 are k / nSim (k: number of simulations below the observation; ties spread uniformly between k / nSim and (k + t) / nSim). They lie on the grid k / nSim, with point masses at 0 and 1, and are therefore not exactly uniform under H0, which becomes visible with many observations or few simulations (#653).

**Decision.** PIT residuals are (k + U (t + 1)) / (nSim + 1), with t the number of simulations tied with the observation and U ~ U(0, 1): the rank of the observation among the nSim + 1 values, with ties broken at random and spread uniformly within the rank cell (randomized PIT, see the references in `?getQuantile`). They are exactly uniform if the observation and the simulations come from the same distribution, also for discrete responses, and never exactly 0 or 1. Outliers are residuals < 1/(nSim + 1) or > nSim/(nSim + 1); under H0, their probability is exactly 1/(nSim + 1) per margin.

**Results** (rejection rates at alpha = 0.05, n = 8000; Monte Carlo SE about 1.3 percentage points):

| Residuals | Response | nSim | KS test | Binomial outlier test | Outlier frequency (expected) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| old | normal | 50 | 100% | 4.7% | 0.039 (0.039) |
| old | Poisson(3) | 50 | 21% | 99.7% | 0.053 (0.039) |
| old | normal | 250 | 11% | 2.0% | 0.0079 (0.0080) |
| old | Poisson(3) | 250 | 5.3% | 31% | 0.0094 (0.0080) |
| new | all four | 50, 250 | 4.7-5.3% | 2.0-5.7% | 0.0079-0.0392 (as expected) |

With the old residuals, the KS test detects the grid for continuous responses with nSim = 50, and the binomial outlier test is far too liberal for count data, where the old residuals fall below 1/(nSim + 1) or above nSim/(nSim + 1) more often than 1/(nSim + 1) per margin. With the new residuals, both tests hold their level. The consequences for `testOutliers()` are in `../outlierTests/`.

**Not changed here.** Residuals with `method = "traditional"` (also used for refit = TRUE) still compare the observation with the empirical distribution of the simulations and can be exactly 0 or 1 (#660).
